How to read the equation: Start with 3.662. Then subtract 0.068 for each 1-point increase in Mental Health, subtract 1.170 if Coaching Support is provided, subtract 0.228 for each 1-point increase in Manager Support, and add 0.227 for each 1-point increase in Workplace Aggression.

Example: For an employee with a Mental Health score of 5, no Coaching Support, Manager Support of 3, and Workplace Aggression of 3:
Turnover Intention = 3.662 − 0.068(5) − 1.170(0) − 0.228(3) + 0.227(3) = 2.94.

Note: These are unstandardised coefficients. They show the estimated change in Turnover Intention for a one-unit change in each factor, while holding the other factors constant. The results show associations, not proof of cause and effect.

Use standardised results to compare the strength and direction of the four predictors’ associations with Turnover Intention, using a comparable scale. It helps identify what matters most.

1. What standardised results mean

The predictors use different scales: Mental Health is scored from 1 to 10, Manager Support and Workplace Aggression from 1 to 5, and Coaching Support is coded 0 (no) or 1 (yes). Standardisation expresses variation in a common unit: the standard deviation (SD).

Think of this as converting different measurements to a common ruler—not a universal importance score. A standard deviation describes how spread out values are in this dataset.

3. How to read the results

Standardised results put the predictors on a comparable scale, making their associations easier to compare.

  • Ignore the sign when comparing size: 0.661 is larger than 0.360, so Coaching Support has the stronger association in this model.
  • A negative value means higher levels of that predictor are associated with lower Turnover Intention, after accounting for the other predictors.
  • A positive value means higher levels are associated with higher Turnover Intention.

Here, Coaching Support has the strongest association with lower Turnover Intention. Workplace Aggression is the only predictor associated with higher Turnover Intention.

These numbers are not percentages or changes in the original Turnover Intention score. To see what a one-point change—or a change from no coaching to coaching—means in score points, use the Unstandardised tab.

4. What standardised results do not mean

  • They do not give the change in Turnover Intention points for a one-point change in a predictor. Use the Unstandardised tab for that.
  • They show adjusted associations, not proof of cause and effect or guaranteed intervention benefits.
  • A coefficient ranking is not a complete decision rule. Consider quality of evidence, feasibility, cost, and other relevant criteria.
